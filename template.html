Apps

Built by Glen Rogers · Be Strong Again · Wisconsin Barbell

I own a gym. I coach people. I have a family. I write books.

And somewhere along the way, I started building apps.

Not because I wanted to become a software company. Not because I was looking for another subscription to sell you.

I built them because I needed them.

I kept running into the same thing: “Why doesn't somebody just make an app that does this?”

Eventually I realized I could. So I did.

Coding has been a passion of mine ever since second grade, when I made a pixelated donkey walk across an IBM screen using MS-DOS. C:\ was amazing to me.

Then the road forked and I went the other way. The gym. Coaching. People.

AI gave me that time back. I can now run parallel lives — coding and gym ownership. And I get to give back in the way that fork took away from me.

These aren't apps I dreamed up in a boardroom. They're things I wished I had. Things my family needed. Things my friends needed. Things my clients needed.

I built them for me. You get to use them too.

And I'm done with the idea that everything has to have a monthly fee, an ad shoved in your face, or some business model attached to it.

Most of these are free. The ones that are free will stay free.

Why I build them

I'm a coach before I'm a programmer.

I spend my days working with people, solving problems, and trying to help somebody become a little stronger than they were yesterday.

That's where these apps came from.

A weekend where I needed a better plan. A cabin where I couldn't remember what was already there. A gym contest where there was no internet. A gymnast whose season shouldn't disappear when the meet was over. A business owner who just wanted to know where the money went without becoming an accountant. A family sitting around a table that needed something better to talk about.

And honestly, some of these started because I was the guy who needed them.

I don't need to build 100 apps. I don't need millions of downloads. I don't need to turn every idea into a subscription.

I just want to build useful things. Things that solve real problems. Things that help people.

Help people get strong. Build something that lasts.

That's really what Be Strong Again has always been about.

One last thing

I'm not trying to build the next big tech company.

I'm trying to build things that matter to somebody.

Sometimes that somebody is me. Sometimes it's my family. Sometimes it's one of my clients. Sometimes it's a friend who has a problem nobody bothered to solve.

And sometimes it might be you.

If something here makes your life a little easier, helps you get a little stronger, helps you remember something important, or gets your family talking again — then it was worth building.

If you need something built

I build software for people and causes I believe in, and I usually do it for nothing. Churches, coaches, small operators, families — anybody who needs one good tool and cannot justify paying somebody to make it. If it is about fitness or about leaving something behind, I am especially interested.

I take on a few of these a year. I would rather finish three things properly than start ten. If that sounds like what you need, write to me and tell me who it is for.
